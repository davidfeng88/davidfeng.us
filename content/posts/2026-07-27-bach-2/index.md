## BWV 8

The title is *Liebster Gott, wenn werd ich sterben?* ("Dearest God, when will I die?"). Death has always been one of humanity's greatest questions. Let's see how Bach approaches it.

### Movement 1: Chorus

{{<youtube c3mud5pxYiU>}}

The accompaniment is instantly recognizable: the recorders keep repeating high notes while the strings pluck away relentlessly, giving the impression that time never stops moving forward. The oboe's melody sounds restless and conflicted, while the choir anxiously asks, "When will I die?" The certainty of death and the uncertainty of its timing intertwine beautifully.

### Movement 4: Bass Aria

{{<youtube dkXBgK5FM7g>}}

The mood changes completely. It's remarkable that a cantata about death can suddenly become so joyful! The virtuosic flute writing is unforgettable, while the text proclaims, "Foolish worries, be gone!" By this point, faith in Christ has overcome the fear of death.

### Movement 6: Chorale

{{<youtube zXKRr-8EEx0>}}

The closing chorale uses a Lutheran hymn melody that Bach's congregation would have immediately recognized. The text entrusts life and death to God and asks Him to lead the believer into eternity. A perfect ending.

But wait—Bach (the GOAT) leaves us one more surprise.

The chorale from the final movement has actually been there all along. Listen carefully to the soprano line in the opening chorus and try to tune out everything else. You'll discover that it's singing the very same melody! I can only admire Bach even more. Starting from such a simple tune, he layers in the other voices, instrumental lines, and rhythmic variations until it becomes something almost unrecognizable—yet somehow even more beautiful.
