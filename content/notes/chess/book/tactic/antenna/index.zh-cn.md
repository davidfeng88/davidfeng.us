## 5 step thinking process

1. Global vision, who has more material, who is attacking. Who has better development, king safety 
2. Analyze the details. Look for signals
3. Identify the theme
4. Look for candidate moves
5. Calculate variations

## Calculation principles 
1. Wide is better than deep
2. Consider your opponent best move 
3. Use forcing moves
4. Step by step snapshot

## Signals

1. King position: squares taken by my pieces? King is stuck?
2. unprotected (or under defended) piece. use queen to fork. setup: chase pieces away so that they are unprotected. 
3. alignment: diagonal, file, rank => pins, skewers, discovered attacks
4. knight forks
5. trapped pieces. note: bishop and knight to take side pawns: might be trapped. if queen tries to take b2 pawn, consider a3, so after Qxb2, we can Na4 to trap the queen. either capture them or put them in jail (bishop to be trapped by pawns).
6. overloaded defender
7. king's defense too far away

## tactic themes
Line / square clearance: I need that square for a piece but it's taken by another of my piece - sacrifice it!
Line / square closing: sacrifice the rook to protect the passed pawn!

### checkmate patterns

- Arabian Mate - N and R
- Epaulette  - Q (opponent's King has two rooks as epaulette)
- Swallow's Tail mate & Dovetail mate - Q (escape squares blocked)
- Damiano's Mate - Queen supported by pawn
- Boden's mate - 2 Bishops. criss-cross
- anastasia's mate
- Pillsbury's Mate - 2B & N
- N & B - B guards the diagonal and N attacks the King
- R & B
- two bishops checkmate - king is in corner

## identify themes to take advantage of a signal

unprotected pieces => Decoy
alignment => pins, skewers, discover attacks, line clearance 
knight fork => decoy. start from my knight, to see where can it go, what squares can it fork, how to lure high value pieces to those squares.
trapped pieces => line closure, line clearance, remove the defender
overloaded defender => remove defender / deflect defender
